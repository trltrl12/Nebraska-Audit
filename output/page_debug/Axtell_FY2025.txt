  auditor's responsibilities for the audit of the financial statements
  our objectives are to obtain reasonable assurance about whether the financial statements as a whole
  are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, and to issue an auditor's report that
  includes our opinions. reasonable assurance is a high level of assurance but is not absolute assurance
  and therefore is not a guarantee that an audit conducted in accordance with generally accepted auditing
  standards and government auditing standards will always detect a material misstatement when it exists.
  the risk of not detecting a material misstatement resulting from fraud is higher than for one resulting from
  error, as fraud may involve collusion, forgery, intentional omissions, misrepresentations, or the override of
  internal control. misstatements are considered material if there is a substantial likelihood that, individually
  or in the aggregate, they would influence the judgment made by a reasonable user based on the financial

  village of axtell, nebraska
  summary schedule of prior year findings and responses
  september 30, 2025
  2024-001 segregation of duties
  due to the size of the village, there is limited segregation of duties over accounting
  functions. the same individual routinely makes deposits, writes checks, and manages the
  general ledger functions. there is a lack of segregation of duties within the cash receipts
  and cash disbursements functions. administrative personnel have access to cash receipts,
  preparing checks, recording the transactions, and making deposits. this is a continued
  finding as noted in item 2025-001.
