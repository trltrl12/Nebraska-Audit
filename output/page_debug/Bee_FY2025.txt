  The Nebraska Auditor of Public Accounts (APA) has reviewed the audit waiver request received from the Village
  of Bee (Village) for the fiscal year ending 2025. That request has been approved.
  While performing, pursuant to Neb. Rev. Stat. § 84-304 (Supp. 2025), the preliminary examination necessary to
  determine whether the audit waiver should be allowed or further audit work would be required, the APA noted
  certain internal control or compliance matters, or other operational issues, within the Village.
  The following information is intended to improve internal controls or result in other operational efficiencies.
  Comments and Recommendations
  1.
  Lack of Formal Policy Regarding Use of Village Equipment
  During our review, we noted that the Village lacked a formal policy governing the use of Village-owned equipment.
  Such omission leaves open the possibility for misuse, abuse, or potential damage of Village property and does not
  provide clear measures for safeguarding of Village assets.
  Neb. Rev. Stat. § 49-14,101.01(2) (Reissue 2021) of the Nebraska Political Accountability and Disclosure Act
  places the following restriction upon the use of property under the “official care and control” of a public official or

  We recommend the Village implement controls over Village assets, including an
  approved Village equipment policy that outlines procedures for the allowable use
  of Village equipment, who is authorized to use such equipment, and disciplinary
  action for misuse of such equipment.
  2.
  Negative Fund Balances
  On Exhibit A of the Village’s audit waiver request form, the APA noted that the Street, Ballroom, and Water funds
  had deficit balances of $264,618, $22,565, and $4,210, respectively, as of September 30, 2025. As
  February 19, 2026, the Village has no plan of action to resolve these deficit fund balances.
  Good internal control and sound accounting practices require procedures to ensure that Village fund balances are
  sufficient to cover fully all disbursements and transfers approved by the Board. Those same procedures should
  ensure also that the Board is apprised regularly of each Village fund balance and, therefore, placed in a position to
  take necessary action to resolve any deficits therein.
  Without such procedures, there is an increased risk of not only insufficient Village fund balances but also the
  resultant inability to cover fully all disbursements and transfers approved by the Board.
  We recommend the Village Board implement procedures to ensure the Board is
  apprised regularly of each Village fund balance and, therefore, placed in a position
  to take necessary action to resolve any deficits therein.
  3.
  Job and Wage Publication
  The APA noted that the Village failed to publish its annual job and wage publication between July 15, 2025, and
  August 15, 2025, as required by State statute. However, the publication was done on December 3, 2025.
  Neb. Rev. Stat. § 19-1102 (Reissue 2022) states, in relevant part, the following:
  Between July 15 and August 15 of each year, the employee job titles and the current annual, monthly, or hourly
  salaries corresponding to such job titles shall be published. Each job title published shall be descriptive and indicative
  of the duties and functions of the position.
  Good internal control requires procedures to ensure that the Village’s annual job and wage publication is published
  between July 15 and August 15 annually, as required by law.
  Without such procedures, there is an increased risk of not only statutory noncompliance but also lack of transparency

  Minutes from The Village of Bee Board of Trustees
  (Unapproved)
  April 9th, 2025
  The general meeting of the Village of Bee Board of Trustees was called to order by Board Chair
  Chris Shipley at 6:56 p m. Shipley, S. Craig, Holloway, Simpson and Odell answered roll call.
  Village Clerk Honeycutt as well as a few residents were also present. Notices were posted in
  three locations. Shipley stated the location of the Open Meetings Act poster. The minutes were
  reviewed, and Holloway made a motion to approve the minutes of the regular meeting
  seconded by S. Craig, all in favor, and the motion carried. Shipley asked for any questions or
  comments concerning the treasurers’ reports. S. Craig made a motion to approve the
  treasurers’ report, seconded by Simpson, all in favor, motion carried.
  WATER & SEWER OPERATOR REPORT
  D. Corner stated for the water side of things it’s pretty good even after the blizzard thing. We
  did end up having to call a generator repair guy who said that the generator is just big enough
  to run the pumphouse, and that it needs to be stored basically empty because the fuel does go
  bad. And on the sewer side, we had to call out Wright Way Drain & Sewer to flush out a main
  between Pine and Ash in the alley. There should be a notice put in the next water bill about
  flushing baby wipes and even the ones that say flushable are not flushable. Another thing on
  the water side, the construction crew for the road work contacted D. Corner and wanted to
  hook up to a hydrant and have it metered or hook it up like any other service. They would be
  bringing in a meter that keeps track of how much water is used. Shipley asked if there was a
  motion. Holloway made a motion to have the water used be metered instead of setting an
  account up, S. Craig seconded the motion, all in favor, motion carried.
  VOLUNTEER FIRE
  R. Corner stated that the fire department has come to an agreement with the Rural Fire Board
  about getting in the truck rotation which is to help with the financing of new fire trucks for the
  county. We are on a six-year rotation, which means that all the trucks will be replaced within a
  six-to-ten-year period. Also, there was one fire call this month. Everything went well except for
  the pump on 62 so that had to be replaced. S. Craig stated that the generator maintenance is
  $1000 to $1200 for service on the generator that ran for three days during the blizzard.
